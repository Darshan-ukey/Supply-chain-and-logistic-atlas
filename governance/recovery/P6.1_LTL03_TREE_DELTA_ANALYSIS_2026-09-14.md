## 1. The decisive mechanism: two frozen rules the reconstruction violated

**Contract §8, the Atomic-Action Rule:** *"A source field labelled `atomicActions` is not automatically atomic... Lexical splitting alone cannot establish executor readiness; missing child-level preconditions, success/failure criteria, evidence, or authority become explicit blockers."*

**Executability Standard:** *"Decomposition stops on an executability criterion, not a fixed hierarchy depth."*

**What the reconstruction actually did, verified directly against the artifact:** every one of the 11 parent groups that has children (`05` through `10`, `15`, `16`, `18`) cites the **exact same three source files**, at file-level granularity, as every one of its own children. I checked this directly (`WD-LTL03-05`, `-07`, `-09`, `-15` and their children all cite identically `road-ltl-v1.5-operational.json` + `-bol-resolution-baseline.json` + `-bol-resolution-claims.json`, nothing more specific per child). **No additional or more specific source material was consulted to justify splitting a parent into children — the split came from parsing the parent's own name/concept into sub-concepts, not from discovering separately-specified child-level detail.**

This is the precise failure mode Contract §8 exists to prevent, and it explains both deltas at once:

- **The 64→43 unit gap** is decomposition depth exceeding what the source actually specified at child granularity.
- **The 38→14 readiness gap** is a direct consequence: my 2026-09-14 correction then had to *manufacture* trigger/evidence/output-state content for each of these lexically-created children to satisfy the schema, because the source never specified that detail at that granularity to begin with. I want to be direct about this: **that correction was grounded (nothing it stated contradicted or invented facts beyond the four source files), but grounded content can still be manufactured-to-fit rather than genuinely present at generation time.** Being able to write a plausible trigger for a unit is not the same as that unit having been independently specified as ready by the frozen source. This is exactly Priority Question #2 from the reconciliation handoff, and the honest answer is: **the majority of the 38 ready leaves rely on this kind of after-the-fact synthesis**, not on detail that was structurally present before either the initial reconstruction or my correction touched the tree.

## 3. The real driver: readiness content that did not exist before enrichment

Applying the stricter historical standard the reconciliation handoff asks for — *"leaves marked ready using detail added during the 2026-09-14 correction rather than detail present in the frozen pre-P6.1 source at compile time"* — and testing each ready leaf against a simple question: **is this leaf's trigger/evidence/authority stated explicitly, per-unit, in the frozen source — or is it inferred from the unit's position in a sequence I (or the reconstruction) constructed?**

For 11 of the 15 parent-with-children groups (05, 06, 07, 08, 09, 10, 15, 16, 17, 18, 19), the *shape* is source-defensible (per §2) but the *per-child* trigger/evidence/output-state was not independently specified by source — only the **parent-level** concept and task-level fields were. Under the Executability Standard's own stop rule (*"stops on an executability criterion"*), the correct historical outcome for most of these families was very likely **to stop decomposition at the parent**, not recurse into named-but-underspecified children — then classify that single parent-level leaf as ready or blocked based on whether the task-level source alone was sufficient.

If the 11 children-bearing groups (05, 06, 07, 08, 09, 10, 15, 16, 17, 18, 19 — currently 3+2+3+3+6+4+3+3+2+2+2 = **33 children**) each collapsed to one leaf apiece instead, that is **33 → 11, a reduction of 22 units** — which lands almost exactly on the required **-21** delta. This fits the evidence far better than the pure-shape analysis in §2 alone, and it is consistent with what I independently verified in §1: parents and children share identical, file-level sourceRefs, meaning no additional specificity was actually discovered by splitting.

**I want to be precise about confidence here.** This is the best-fitting explanation I can construct from the frozen sources, and the arithmetic fit is strong, but I have not independently reconstructed a 43-unit tree to prove it exactly reproduces the historical shape — doing so is explicitly the next, separate step, not this one.

## 5. Proposed deterministic compiler/generator rule corrections

1. **Source-specificity gate on every split.** Before creating a child unit, the compiler must verify the child's `sourceRefs` would differ in *content*, not merely be inherited unchanged from the parent. If a candidate split would leave every child citing exactly the same source material as the parent with no additional per-child specification, **do not split** — keep the parent as the leaf and classify it directly.
2. **Explicit "one family" veto.** Where source text explicitly describes a group of fields/concepts as one associated/conditional object family (Contract §8's own example category), the compiler must treat that language as a hard constraint against fragmentation, not a hint. This single rule would have prevented the Group 09 case outright.
3. **No sequence-inferred triggers.** A unit's `trigger` must be traceable to an explicit source statement about *that unit's own* entry condition — not synthesized from "this follows the previous step" positional reasoning. If no such statement exists, `trigger` must stay absent, and per Contract §6.2/§6.3 the leaf cannot be `EXECUTOR_READY` on that basis alone — it must fail closed to `BLOCKED_BY_KNOWLEDGE_GAP`.
4. **Enrichment-order invariant.** Executability content (trigger/atomicActions/evidenceRequirements/outputState) must be established *before* or *at* the compile step that assigns `executorReadiness.status`, never added afterward to justify a status already assigned. This is the specific ordering violation my 2026-09-14 correction fell into, and it should become an explicit compiler-spec rule, not just a QA check.
5. **Workflow-step-list is structure, not detail.** An explicitly enumerated step list (like the 14-step `resolutionWorkflow`) licenses creating one unit per named step (shape), but the step name alone does not satisfy the per-unit evidence/authority requirement — each such unit still needs its own source-grounded trigger/evidence or it fails closed, per rule 3.

## 6. Expected impact on LTL-03 if these rules are applied in a fresh regeneration

Applying rules 1–2 alone: unit count falls from 64 toward roughly **59** (Group 09 six→two, Group 08 three→two). Applying rule 3 additionally, to the 33 children across groups 05–10/15–19 whose triggers were sequence-inferred rather than source-explicit: most of these fail closed to knowledge-gap rather than remaining ready, which would move the tree toward something in the neighborhood of the historical **40s-unit / high-single-or-low-double-digit ready** shape — **not asserted as an exact target**, only as the directionally expected consequence of applying the rules honestly.

## 7. Is this rule set generic enough to become the permanent `CANONICAL_WORK_DECOMPOSITION_COMPILER_SPEC_V1`?

**Yes, with one caveat.** Rules 1, 3, 4 and 5 are fully generic — they reference no Road-LTL- or LTL-03-specific concept and follow directly from the already-frozen Contract §8 and the Executability Standard's stop criterion; they are really *enforcement mechanisms* for rules that are already frozen, not new policy. Rule 2 (the "one family" veto) is generic in mechanism (treat explicit source language about grouped/conditional fields as a hard constraint) but its trigger condition — recognizing such language — will need domain judgement per source document; I'd recommend codifying it as a required manual-review checkpoint in the compiler spec rather than a fully automatable check.
